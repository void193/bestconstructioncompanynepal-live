What to Look for in a Construction Company in Nepal

Before comparing specific companies, it helps to know what separates a reliable builder from a risky one. These are the basics worth checking regardless of who you're hiring:

  • In-house civil engineering, not just labor. A company with its own structural/civil engineers can catch design problems before they become expensive site problems — you shouldn't have to hire a separate engineering firm to check the builder's own work.
  • A visible, verifiable project history. Ask to see completed projects, ideally ones you can visit or that have documented locations — not just renders.
  • One team for design through handover. Coordinating a separate architect, engineer and contractor yourself adds real friction and cost. Companies that handle architecture, engineering, construction and interiors together reduce that overhead.
  • A real cost estimate before you commit. Reasonable builders can walk you through cement, steel, sand, aggregate and per-square-foot costs before breaking ground, not just after.
  • Local presence in your district. A company with an actual office (not just a phone number) in or near Kathmandu, Chitwan or Bharatpur will generally respond faster and know local permitting and material sourcing better than one operating remotely.
